### 1. Short-Form: "The catchment trap"

[HOOK: 0 to 3 seconds]
Being in the catchment doesn't mean your kid gets a seat.

[PROBLEM: 5 to 10 seconds]
Most families moving to Ottawa pick the house first. Then, right before the offer goes firm, someone asks, "Wait. Which school does this actually feed into?"

[SOLUTION: 15 to 30 seconds]
Here's the thing. Ottawa has four school boards: English public, English Catholic, French public and French Catholic. Every address maps to a different school on each one. And in the fast-growing south end, the boundary map is only half the story. The other half is capacity. So before you fall for a kitchen, call the board and ask two questions. What school is this address zoned for? And is that school over capacity right now?

[PROOF: 10 to 15 seconds]
Look at Vimy Ridge Public School in Findlay Creek. It was built for 674 students, and by 2022 it had 17 portables in the yard. Riverside South went without a public high school until Riverside South Secondary opened in September 2025.

[CTA: 5 seconds]
Pick the school, then the house. More at yourhomeinottawa.ca.

ON-SCREEN TEXT:
1. Catchment is not a guaranteed seat
2. 4 school boards. 4 different answers.
3. Ask: zoned school + current capacity

B-ROLL: Exterior of a south-end elementary school with portables (shoot from the public road, no students in frame). Riverside South Secondary exterior. A Findlay Creek street of newer homes. A phone screen showing the OCDSB school locator.

Word count / runtime: about 185 words / about 70 seconds
YouTube Shorts Title: Ottawa Schools: The Catchment Trap Most Families Miss
Facebook Video Title: Moving to Ottawa with kids? Check this before you firm up

Sources:
- OCDSB interim accommodation document (Vimy Ridge capacity of 674 pupil places)
- Ottawa Citizen, 2022 (17 portables at Vimy Ridge)
- riversidesouthss.ocdsb.ca (opened September 2025, Grades 7 to 10, adding a grade each year)

Flag: The portable count is from 2022. Say "by 2022" exactly as written, or get a current count from the OCDSB before filming.

### 2. Short-Form: "$623,500 in Ottawa: three places I'd start"

[HOOK: 0 to 3 seconds]
Ottawa's benchmark home price is $623,500. So where does that actually get you?

[PROBLEM: 5 to 10 seconds]
If you're moving here, that number feels like it should buy a house anywhere in the city. It doesn't. Same budget, very different homes, depending on where you land.

[SOLUTION: 15 to 30 seconds]
So here's where I'd start. Findlay Creek, if you want newer construction and quick access to the airport and Bank Street. Riverside South, now that the Trillium Line runs to Limebank, if you want transit without living downtown. And Kanata, if you work in the Kanata North tech park and want an established neighbourhood with the 417 right there. At this budget, think townhomes and some semis, not detached.

[PROOF: 10 to 15 seconds]
That $623,500 is the September 2026 benchmark from the Ottawa Real Estate Board, down 2.2% from August. Right now, buyers have more choice than they've had in a while.

[CTA: 5 seconds]
Tell me where you work and I'll tell you where I'd look. More at yourhomeinottawa.ca.

ON-SCREEN TEXT:
1. $623,500 = Ottawa benchmark (OREB, Sept 2026)
2. Findlay Creek / Riverside South / Kanata
3. Start with where you work

B-ROLL: Townhome rows in Findlay Creek. The Limebank O-Train station. Kanata North tech park signage. Greg walking a street.

Word count / runtime: about 190 words / about 70 seconds
YouTube Shorts Title: What $623,500 Buys in Ottawa: 3 Neighbourhoods to Start With
Facebook Video Title: Moving to Ottawa on a $650K budget? Start here

Sources:
- OREB / WOWA September 2026 report (benchmark $623,500, down 2.2% from August)

Flag: Greg, confirm "townhomes and some semis" matches what's actually selling in these three areas at this price. Swap in TCMG's core areas if they differ.

### 3. Short-Form: "Do the commute before you do the offer"

[HOOK: 0 to 3 seconds]
A 15-minute drive on Sunday. 35 minutes on a Tuesday at 8.

[PROBLEM: 5 to 10 seconds]
Federal workers are back in the office four days a week, and I keep hearing the same thing. "The listing said it's 20 minutes to downtown." Honestly? Listings measure distance, not rush hour.

[SOLUTION: 15 to 30 seconds]
So before any offer, do one test. Pick a weekday. Leave from the house at the time you'd actually leave. Drive your real route, or take the real transit trip, door to desk. If there's a second way home, like the O-Train, a bike path or a different bridge, test that too. One drive settles more debates than any listing photo.

[PROOF: 10 to 15 seconds]
[GREG: add one anonymised client story here. Example shape: "Last spring a couple we worked with fell for a house in ___. They did the drive at 7:45 on a Wednesday. It was ___ minutes. They bought in ___ instead."]

[CTA: 5 seconds]
Tell me where you work and I'll tell you which neighbourhoods pass the test. More at yourhomeinottawa.ca.

ON-SCREEN TEXT:
1. Sunday drive ≠ Tuesday commute
2. Test it: weekday, rush hour, door to desk
3. Always have a second way home

B-ROLL: Dashcam or phone timelapse of a real rush-hour drive, with a clock overlay. The Queensway in traffic. The O-Train pulling in. The canal pathway with bikes.

Word count / runtime: about 175 words, plus the client story / about 70 seconds
YouTube Shorts Title: The Ottawa Commute Test to Do Before You Buy
Facebook Video Title: Back in the office 4 days a week? Do this before you buy

Flag: Before filming, confirm the four-day office requirement for federal workers is still the current rule. Then film your own drive to go with the hook. That real footage is what made Eric's version work.
